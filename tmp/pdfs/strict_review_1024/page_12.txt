Simulations over five training seeds and user populations
of 20–40 showed that, compared with PDQN, HAN–PDQN
reduced average successful-task delay, deadline violation rate,
and energy per successful task by 2.16%, 21.26%, and 5.72%,
respectively. These task-level improvements were accompa-
nied by reductions in blocked user-time, total terminal energy,
and handover frequency. The results demonstrate the effective-
ness of the proposed framework in balancing task reliability,
processing delay, and terminal energy across the evaluated
user populations. Future work will extend the framework
to jointly allocate communication and computing resources
and investigate policy transfer across constellation and traffic
configurations.
