Problem (22) couples discrete satellite selection with contin-
uous workload allocation. A selected satellite determines the
link and computing conditions, while the offloading fraction
changes the contention experienced by other users. Moreover,
residual workloads and handover-induced reassignment carry
these effects across slots. Consequently, effective decisions
require both a representation of multi-user contention and an
evaluation of the long-term value of each satellite–fraction
pair. These requirements motivate the HAN–PDQN framework
developed next.
IV. PROPOSEDHAN–PDQN FRAMEWORK
A. Framework Overview
To address (22), we develop HAN–PDQN, which connects
a representation of multi-user contention to parameterized
satellite and offloading decisions. As illustrated in Fig. 2, the
controller constructs a heterogeneous graph from user demand,
satellite resources, and their serving and visibility relations.
HAN aggregates these measurements into a user representation
that distinguishes established resource sharing from potential
competition through overlapping satellite alternatives.
The representation is combined with the user’s local task
and candidate-satellite observations. Conditioned on this input,
PDQN generates an offloading fraction for each satellite action
and evaluates the resulting satellite–fraction pairs. All users
share the decision-network parameters, while their observa-
tions and feasible candidates produce different actions. The
joint requests are then executed under the service rules in
Section II, yielding task outcomes and the next network
snapshot. Sections IV-B and IV-C develop the representation
and decision mechanisms, respectively; Section IV-D describes
training and online execution.
B. Heterogeneous Graph-Based Contention Representation
At slott, we constructG t = (V,E t,X t,E t), whereV
contains user and satellite nodes,E t contains typed edges,
andX t andE t collect node and edge features. User features
describe location, connection status, task demand, urgency, and
backlog. Satellite features describe location, MEC utilization,
queue occupancy, associated users, and available computing
resources. Visibility edges carry distance, elevation, SNR,
reference rate, and remaining visibility; serving edges iden-
tify existing associations and their service duration. Nearby-
user and ISL edges provide geographical and constellation-
neighborhood information. User–satellite relations are repre-
sented in both directions.
