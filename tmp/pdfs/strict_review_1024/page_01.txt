Joint Handover and Partial Task Offloading in LEO
Satellite Edge Networks via Heterogeneous
Graph-Based Parameterized Reinforcement Learning
Author Name
Abstract—Integrating low Earth orbit (LEO) satellites with
edge computing extends computation services to areas with
limited terrestrial coverage and to terminals with constrained
local resources. However, satellite motion continually changes
link quality, satellite visibility, and remaining service time, while
multiple users compete for limited onboard communication and
computing resources. For divisible tasks, a user must select a
serving satellite and determine a continuous offloading ratio
matched to that satellite’s link, resource, and contention con-
ditions. We therefore formulate joint serving-satellite selection
and partial task offloading as a sequential decision problem with
a parameterized hybrid action space. To solve this problem, we
propose HAN–PDQN, which combines a heterogeneous graph
attention network (HAN) with a parameterized deep Q-network
(PDQN). A heterogeneous graph distinguishes user and satel-
lite nodes as well as serving and visibility relations. Relation-
specific meta-path aggregation and hierarchical attention capture
established resource sharing and potential contention among
users with overlapping satellite alternatives. Conditioned on this
relational representation, local task information, and candidate-
satellite features, PDQN generates a continuous offloading ratio
for each feasible satellite action and estimates the long-term value
of the resulting satellite–ratio pair. The same contention context
therefore affects both how much workload is assigned to each
candidate and whether that candidate is selected. HAN–PDQN
consequently supports coordinated serving-satellite selection and
partial task offloading in dynamic multi-user LEO satellite edge
networks.Simulations across five training seeds and network sizes
of 20–40 users show that, compared with PDQN, HAN–PDQN
reduces average successful-task delay, energy per successful task,
and the deadline violation rate by 2.16%, 5.72%, and 21.26%,
respectively, while increasing the task success rate by 2.12%.
Index Terms—Heterogeneous graph attention, joint handover
and offloading, LEO satellite edge computing, parameterized
deep reinforcement learning, partial task offloading.
I. INTRODUCTION
Ubiquitous connectivity and computing are important objec-
tives of sixth-generation (6G) networks. However, terrestrial
infrastructure cannot consistently provide edge services in
remote regions, maritime environments, and other areas with
limited network coverage. Satellite edge computing can com-
plement terrestrial systems by extending communication and
computation services to these areas [1]. In particular, low Earth
orbit (LEO) satellites offer shorter propagation distances than
satellites in higher orbits and can host onboard edge servers to
process tasks from ground terminals. These capabilities make
LEO satellite edge computing a promising option for users
with limited local computing resources.
Providing sustained computing services in LEO networks
requires coordinated decisions on satellite association and
workload allocation. Satellite motion continuously changes
the visible satellite set, link quality, and remaining service
time, making handovers necessary to maintain service [2].
Meanwhile, multiple users share the limited uplink bandwidth
and computing resources of each satellite. For divisible tasks,
a user must decide both which satellite to select and what frac-
tion of its workload to offload, while processing the remaining
fraction locally in parallel. A suitable offloading ratio depends
on the selected satellite’s link conditions, computing capacity,
queued workload, and remaining visibility. Conversely, users’
offloading ratios change resource occupancy and the execu-
tion conditions experienced by other users. Under stochastic
task arrivals, unfinished workloads carry these effects across
time slots, while handovers may interrupt service and require
workload migration. Serving-satellite selection and partial task
offloading must therefore be considered jointly: the former
determines remote execution conditions, whereas the latter
determines the workload assigned to those conditions.
Existing studies provide two complementary foundations
for addressing this coupled decision problem. Graph-based
resource management and satellite handover methods use
relational representations to organize network interactions and
support access decisions [3], [4]. Such representations are use-
ful for identifying service opportunities and resource-sharing
relationships, but satellite selection alone does not specify the
continuous task fraction to assign to the selected server. Mean-
while, satellite edge computing studies have investigated joint
access selection, offloading, and resource allocation [5], [6],
including multi-agent and hierarchical reinforcement learning
approaches to dynamic multi-user control [7]–[9]. Parameter-
ized reinforcement learning further supports discrete decisions
with associated continuous controls [10]. For example, an
existing satellite formulation selects among local, satellite, and
cloud execution modes while allocating computing resources
and transmission power [11]. These advances motivate a more
specific design question for partial offloading: how should
the relationships among competing users inform both the
workload proposed for each candidate satellite and the value
of selecting that satellite? Addressing this question requires in-
tegrating relational contention modeling with satellite-specific
discrete–continuous control.
