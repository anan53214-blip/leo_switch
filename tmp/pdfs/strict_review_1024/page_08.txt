V. PERFORMANCEEVALUATION
A. Experimental Setup and Evaluation Protocol
We evaluate HAN–PDQN in a multi-user LEO satellite
edge computing network comprising 66 satellites distributed
across six orbital planes. The orbital altitude and inclination
are 550 km and53 ◦, respectively. Ground users are distributed
around(39.9 ◦N,116.4 ◦E), and the population varies from
20 to 40 users. User locations and orbital starting times are
resampled at episode initialization. Each episode contains 512
slots, with a slot duration of 1 s. Tables II and III summarize
the system and training parameters, respectively.
Each user generates a task independently with probabil-
ity 0.35 per slot. Tasks are classified as light, medium,
and heavy with probabilities 0.3, 0.5, and 0.2. For light
tasks, the input size, computation demand, and deadline
are uniformly sampled from[1,3]Mbit,[0.3,0.8]Gcycle,
and[1.5,3]s, respectively. The corresponding ranges are
[5,10]Mbit,[1.2,2.5]Gcycle, and[2.5,5.5]s for medium
tasks, and[12,25]Mbit,[3,7]Gcycle, and[4,8]s for
heavy tasks. Each MEC provides a total processing rate of
10 Gcycle/s through two 5-GHz cores. The utility weights
are(ω D, ωE, ωI, ωF, ωL) = (0.60,0.40,0.30,0.20,0.05), with
Eref = 1J.
We compare HAN–PDQN with six benchmarks:
•HAN+MAPPO: A graph-assisted multi-agent proximal
policy optimization controller for joint satellite selection
and offloading.
•MAPPO: A multi-agent proximal policy optimization
controller using raw user, task, and candidate-satellite
observations.
•MADDPG: A multi-agent deep deterministic policy gra-
dient controller using raw observations to generate satel-
lite and offloading decisions.
•PDQN: A parameterized Q-learning controller using raw
observations without HAN representations.
•Min-Distance: A distance-based policy that requests the
nearest admissible satellite under the handover conditions
and requests full offloading. It retains the current associ-
ation when no shorter admissible alternative is available.
•Full-Local: A policy that processes all tasks locally and
issues no handover requests.
All methods operate under the same task, communication,
MEC service, and admission models.
For each user population, the learning methods are trained
with seeds 42–46 and an interaction budget of1.5×10 5
environment steps per run. Table III reports the principal
training settings. HAN–PDQN and PDQN use replay batch
sizes of 512 and 128, respectively. Periodic model evaluation
is performed every 25,000 environment steps for the PDQN
and MAPPO variants, and every ten episodes for MADDPG.
Checkpoints are selected according to their mean evaluation
return.
Each selected policy is evaluated over five episodes per
training seed, using matched scenario seeds across methods.
Performance metrics are first averaged over these episodes and
then summarized across the five training seeds. Shaded regions
and error bars represent 95% bootstrap confidence intervals at
the training-seed level.
