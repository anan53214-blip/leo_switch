We count a task as successful only when its required
workload completes without branch termination and its eval-
uated delay satisfiesT i,n ≤τ i,n. Tasks exceeding this limit,
including those expiring in user or MEC queues, contribute
to deadline violations. We record disconnection-induced ter-
minations separately, while tasks unresolved at the evaluation
horizon remain pending. For an expired or terminated task,
we record its outcome rather than assign a normal completion
delay.
In evaluating performance, we normalize success and
deadline-violation counts by the number of generated tasks
and average successful-task delay over successful tasks only.
Energy per successful task divides the total terminal energy
of all dispatched workloads by the successful-task count;
its numerator includes energy charged to unsuccessful and
unresolved tasks.
D. Handover and Service Model
To maintain service as the constellation moves, a user
may retain its association or request a handover to another
visible satellite. Such a request is enabled when the user is
disconnected, its current link becomes invalid, the remaining
visibility falls belowv trig, or the serving MEC reaches its
utilization threshold. The target must meet the minimum
elevation requirement, remaining visibility thresholdv min, and
reference uplink SNR thresholdΓ min, with the reference SNR
evaluated over total uplink bandwidthB s.

user’s unfinished remote workloads. Specifically, ifmi(t)tasks
