D. Service Continuity and Operating Costs
Fig. 6 complements the task-level results by examining
service interruption, aggregate terminal energy, and handover
activity. These metrics reveal how different association and
offloading policies balance connection availability against the
conditions under which tasks are executed.
In Fig. 6(a), HAN–PDQN has lower mean blocked user-time
than PDQN at every population. At 30 users, the values are
284.08 and 298.12 user-s per episode, respectively. The cumu-
lative quantity increases with population, partly because more
users contribute to the total observation time. Min-Distance
yields a slightly lower value of 276.32 user-s at 30 users, but its
deadline violation rate in Fig. 5(b) is much higher. Connection
availability therefore needs to be considered together with the
processing conditions and workload assigned to the selected
satellite.
Fig. 6(b) shows that HAN–PDQN also maintains the lowest
mean total terminal energy over the evaluated populations.
At 30 users, it consumes 6.18 kJ per episode, compared
with 6.37 kJ for PDQN, 8.41 kJ for Min-Distance, and
11.26 kJ for Full-Local. Total energy measures the system’s
accumulated terminal expenditure, whereas Fig. 5(c) measures
the expenditure per successful task. The improvement in both
quantities shows that the energy-efficiency gain accompanies
a reduction in aggregate terminal consumption.
The handover results in Fig. 6(c) show a different ordering
among the methods. At 30 users, HAN–PDQN performs
0.606 handovers per user-minute, compared with 0.659 for
PDQN, 0.192 for HAN+MAPPO, and 0.198 for Min-Distance.
Thus, HAN–PDQN obtains its task-performance advantage
with fewer handovers than PDQN, while remaining more
active in satellite reassociation than HAN+MAPPO and Min-
Distance. The lower switching activity of the latter policies
is accompanied by higher task delay, deadline violations, and
energy per successful task. This comparison reflects the role
of the long-term objective in balancing immediate switching
costs against subsequent task-service conditions.
Using the same across-population averaging convention as
in Section V-C, HAN–PDQN reduces mean blocked user-
time, total terminal energy, and handover frequency relative
to PDQN by 7.85%, 3.19%, and 14.82%, respectively. These
operating-cost reductions accompany the task-level improve-
ments, linking the quality of completed service to the associ-
ation and workload decisions that sustain it.
VI. CONCLUSION
In this paper, we investigated joint handover and partial
task offloading in multi-user LEO satellite edge networks.
We formulated satellite selection and continuous workload
allocation as a sequential decision problem and developed
HAN–PDQN to account for dynamic connectivity and shared
resources. HAN distinguishes established contention through
serving relations from potential contention through common
satellite alternatives. The resulting representation conditions
both the offloading fraction generated for each candidate and
the long-term value of the corresponding satellite–fraction pair.
